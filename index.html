Stage 02 — Price

Never quote a number you can't defend.

Under-pricing is the quiet killer of a bookkeeping practice, and the fee line is where most people freeze. Evisor turns the job in front of you into three options you can present, checks every one against your own cost floor, and hands you a client-ready quote that drops straight into the proposal.

  • Three options, not one number. Clients who are given a choice buy the middle one. Clients who are given a number negotiate it.

  • A floor you never drop below. Built from your hourly cost and the real scope, so a busy month can't turn into an unprofitable client.

  • Market benchmarks on Advisor. What practices of your size and niche are actually charging — which is the number you need in January, when you raise your fees.

This is the most-used tool inside Evisor. It's also the one nobody expects a document product to have.

Worth knowing

Two owners can both sign the same letter. A married couple filing jointly, two partners in a practice, a director and a co-director. Ignition's Solo and Core plans allow one signature per proposal. Co-signers are included on every paid Evisor plan.

On the record

Every signature is captured with the disclosure version shown, the presentation timestamp, the signer's IP, and a document hash recorded before and after signing. Content and signatures are encrypted at rest. You can download any signed PDF, any time, forever.

The alternatives

You've already looked at the others.

They're good products. They're also built for firms that already have a full book, and priced accordingly. Three differences worth checking yourself.

Against Ignition
Their entry plan asks you to be under a revenue ceiling and to connect Xero or QuickBooks with payments switched on — a brand-new practice can fail that test on day one. And they take a percentage of every payment your client makes. Evisor has no revenue test, needs no integration, and takes nothing from your fees.
Against DocuSign
A signature tool, billed per envelope, that knows nothing about the client, the fee, or what happens next January. Evisor signs the document and also finds the prospect, prices the work, collects the payment, and renews it.
Against TaxDome
Billed per user per year, upfront, with weeks of configuration before it does anything useful. Excellent once you're at scale. Punishing when you have four clients and need a letter out this afternoon. Your first letter goes out today.

And there's one column no suite fills at all: finding the client. Every one of them begins after you already have the prospect.

Every tool for winning and keeping clients was either built for big firms, priced like it, or started at the paperwork. None of them helped with the part that actually keeps a new bookkeeper awake — finding the next client.
